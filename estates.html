How it works

Measure first. Specify what matters.

A sauna is a product. A wellness room is an amenity. Longevity architecture treats the residence itself as the unit of work. The process begins by understanding the environment, then deciding what deserves attention first.

Step 01

Begin by making the invisible visible.

MAVI’s Snapshot uses available address-level environmental information to provide an initial view of conditions around the residence. It is an entry point, not a medical assessment or a substitute for project-specific testing.

The MAVI Blueprint

A plan you can act on.

The air at the pillow. The water at every fixture. The light after sunset. The sound that follows you into the bedroom. The materials surrounding your family. These conditions are easy to overlook because they are part of the background.

MAVI’s Blueprint evaluates a residence across 129 factors, then organizes the findings into a ranked plan. It documents the current state, identifies priority interventions, and connects recommendations to cited research and applicable supplier or specification references.

From information to implementation

MAVI produces the Blueprint. For accepted Estate projects, Crewzz coordinates applicable recommendations with architecture, engineering, interiors, materials, systems, controls, procurement, and the agreed verification plan.

MAVI was founded by Kas Bordier. Its published framework draws on more than 200 cited studies, and its longevity architecture work with Kelly Hoppen CBE was featured by Financial Times House & Home in May 2026. Research informs the specification. No medical outcome is promised.

The MAVI framework

Ten domains. One connected residence.

The body does not experience air, light, sound, temperature, and space in isolation. MAVI’s Blueprint reads them together. Crewzz uses the approved priorities as a coordination layer for the accepted project scope, room by room and system by system.

  1. 01

    Air

    Ventilation, filtration, pollutants, humidity, and the air moving through occupied rooms.

  2. 02

    Light

    Daylight, electric light, glare, controls, and changing light conditions across the day.

  3. 03

    Water

    Water used for drinking, cooking, bathing, fixtures, filtration, and distribution.

  4. 04

    Sound

    Exterior noise, equipment, room acoustics, privacy, and unwanted sound transfer.

  5. 05

    Thermal comfort

    Temperature, stability, airflow, humidity, zoning, and personal comfort.

  6. 06

    Materials

    Interior finishes, furnishings, adhesives, coatings, and documented material choices.

  7. 07

    EMF and technology

    Wiring, connected systems, devices, controls, and electromagnetic conditions.

  8. 08

    Mind

    Spatial cues, visual calm, privacy, restoration, and the emotional experience of home.

  9. 09

    Microbiology

    Moisture, surfaces, ventilation, maintenance conditions, and the home’s microbial environment.

  10. 10

    Habits

    How planning, storage, circulation, light, and controls support everyday routines.

Straight answers

Before you apply.

What is longevity architecture?

Longevity architecture applies research-informed environmental considerations to the design and operation of a home. It can include air, water, light, sound, temperature, materials, technology, and the daily patterns the residence supports.

How is this different from adding wellness amenities?

A wellness amenity is an individual feature. Longevity architecture treats the residence itself as the unit of work and coordinates environmental decisions across rooms, systems, materials, controls, and daily routines.

Who is MAVI?

MAVI is a longevity architecture consultancy founded by Kas Bordier. Its published framework organizes residential environmental considerations across ten domains and 129 factors, with recommendations connected to cited research.

Who can apply?

Available to Crewzz Estate clients only. Projects are reviewed for fit, geography, timing, specialist availability, and current capacity.

Is this medical care?

No. This is residential planning, coordination, and educational information. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, or a guarantee of sleep, recovery, longevity, or any other health outcome.

Can this be introduced into an existing residence?

Potentially. Feasibility depends on the home, existing systems, project scope, specialist input, budget, and the extent of renovation being considered.
