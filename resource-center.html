Why this exists

The expensive lesson is the one that arrives too late.

A major home project can place the homeowner between architects, designers, engineers, contractors, trades, vendors, inspectors, contracts, invoices, and hundreds of decisions.

Each person may understand one part. You are still the person approving the whole.

The Crewzz Resource Center turns construction language into homeowner language. It is free to read and designed to help you recognize the questions that deserve a clear answer before you approve the work.

You do not need to become the contractor. You do need to know what you are authorizing.

Straight answers

Questions homeowners often ask too late.

These are general answers, not substitutes for project-specific advice. Use them to see the issue clearly, then ask the appropriate professional to address your property, contract, and jurisdiction.

Showing all 9 answers

What should I review before comparing construction bids?
Bids and contracts

First confirm that every bidder is pricing the same project. Compare the drawings, specifications, scope, exclusions, allowances, schedule assumptions, and contract terms behind each total.

A lower total is not necessarily a lower price when necessary work is missing, deferred, or carried as an allowance that does not match what you expect to select.

What is an allowance, and why does it matter?
Costs

An allowance is a placeholder amount for an item or scope that has not been fully selected or priced. The final cost is later reconciled against that placeholder.

The number matters, but so does the assumption behind it. Ask what quantity, quality, labor, tax, delivery, and related work the allowance includes.

What should a change order explain before I approve it?
During construction

A change order should identify what changed, why it changed, the supporting cost, the effect on the schedule, and the work or contract terms being modified.

Do not approve only a number. Approve a documented change you understand.

Who should be involved before design or construction begins?
Before you start

The answer depends on the project, but early planning may require an architect, interior designer, engineer, surveyor, contractor, permitting specialist, cost advisor, or other qualified consultant.

The essential step is to define who owns each decision, what information must be produced, and when one discipline must review the work of another.

When is a design ready to approve?
Design decisions

A design is ready to approve when the decision is clear enough for its next use. A concept approval establishes direction. A construction approval requires much more detail.

Before work begins, confirm that critical dimensions, materials, systems, interfaces, selections, and unresolved items are documented at the level the project team needs.

Who is responsible for checking the work?
Construction

Responsibility depends on the contract, jurisdiction, discipline, and stage of work. Contractors, architects, engineers, inspectors, consultants, and homeowners may each have different duties.

Those duties and planned review points should be documented before the work begins. Government inspection does not necessarily replace contractual quality review or professional observation.

When should wellness, safety, and security enter the design process?
Estate systems

As early as the systems or conditions may affect architecture, engineering, utilities, equipment space, wiring, access, privacy, or circulation.

These decisions require qualified specialists. Crewzz helps qualified Estate clients explore coordination with appropriate professionals based on project fit.

What should I receive when construction is complete?
After construction

The handover record should reflect the project and agreement. It may include approved drawings, specifications, equipment information, warranties, manuals, finish records, service contacts, inspections, open items, and maintenance requirements.

A well-built home still needs to be maintained. Organize the record while the people who created it still know where everything is.

When should I stop reading and call a qualified professional?
Know the limit

Seek project-specific advice when a decision affects legal rights, contracts, liens, payment disputes, structural or engineering matters, health, safety, code compliance, taxes, insurance, or material financial exposure.
